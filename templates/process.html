The handover process: before and after

The software only works if the process around it does. This is the process the tracker is built around, and how I'd prove it helps.

Before (from the brief)

  1. Parts leave Headers or Sheet Metal on a trolley or pickup. Nothing is written down in any structured way.
  2. They arrive at the other plant. Nobody confirms how many.
  3. A part goes missing, or a plant says it never got it.
  4. It's settled by conflicting memory, phone calls and searching, while the build waits.

Result: no record to check, and lost production time.

After (this tracker)

  1. Send (sender, about 30 s): pick the job order and your plant; the parts it still needs are prefilled; adjust to what's on the trolley; print the slip.
  2. Move: the slip travels with the parts. It has a QR code and no quantities.
  3. Receive (storekeeper, about 30 s): scan the QR, count each part without seeing the sent number (blind count), confirm.
  4. Exceptions surface by themselves: any difference becomes a discrepancy for both plants; anything not received in {{ SLA_HOURS|qty }} hours goes red; either plant can flag "not received".
  5. Settle with the record: Settle a dispute answers who sent what, who confirmed it and who last had the parts, from an append-only trail.

How I'd roll it out and prove it works

  1. Week 0, baseline: walk the route with the people who move the parts. Count, by hand, disputes per week and hours lost searching on one route (Sheet Metal to Coil Assembly).
  2. Weeks 1 and 2, pilot: that one route only, with the two or three people who send and receive. Fix what they complain about first.
  3. Measure: % of receipts confirmed within {{ SLA_HOURS|qty }} hours, transit time, discrepancy rate per department and time to settle, on the Insights page, against the week-0 count.
  4. Then roll out to the other routes, and post to SAP once the counts are trusted.

Where AI fits, and where it doesn't

Not in the record. The trail settles disputes between plants, so it stays rule-based and exact. A guess has no place in it.

At the edges, where it saves typing or reading:

  • Turn a photo of today's paper challan, or a WhatsApp message, into a draft send for a person to confirm.
  • A short daily summary for plant heads: what's overdue, which route keeps coming up short, what's still open.
  • Read the free-text notes on discrepancies ("dented corner", "left on rack") to find the repeat causes, the way an 8D would.

The Insights flags already do the rule-based version of the second item. This prototype was built with AI coding tools; I directed the design and checked the result.
